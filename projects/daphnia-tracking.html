Garry Sverlov / Daphnia tracking

Daphnia tracking at Daphis

Computer Vision Intern, Daphis Therapeutics, Apr - Jun 2025. Python, OpenCV, NumPy, pandas, SciPy, filterpy

Daphis Therapeutics is a longevity research startup that studies how compounds affect lifespan using Daphnia, tiny water fleas. Researchers dose separate tanks with different vitamins and film them, and they needed a way to turn that video into numbers they could compare. I was the only developer on that problem, and built the pipeline that does it.

How it works

Finding the animals. Each video is 1280 by 1024 at 20 frames per second, with around 90 Daphnia in frame. MOG2 background subtraction picks out what's moving, a contrast filter throws away shadows, and watershed splitting separates animals that overlap or cross paths.

Keeping track of who's who. A Kalman filter predicts where each animal will be next, and the Hungarian algorithm matches predictions to new detections. The matching cost adds a small penalty for size differences, so two animals passing each other don't swap identities.

Turning tracks into data. I picked the five movement metrics a published Daphnia study found most predictive of longevity: body length, average speed, how much the speed varies, the share of time spent swimming fast, and where in the tank the animal stays. Everything is converted from pixels to millimetres using the tank's known size, and written out as CSVs per animal and per video.

Outcome

The movement data the pipeline produced went into the company's investor pitch, which reached the Y Combinator interview round. I also built the tuning and comparison tools used to set the pipeline's parameters, and handed it off with a rewritten README and pinned dependencies.
